<p>The <em>September 11 Digital Archive</em> uses electronic media to collect, preserve, and present the history of the September 11, 2001 attacks in New York, Virginia, and Pennsylvania and the public responses to them.</p>
<p>Initially funded by a major grant from the <a href="http://www.sloan.org">Alfred P. Sloan Foundation</a> and organized by the <a href="http://www.ashp.cuny.edu">American Social History Project</a> at the City University of New York Graduate Center and the <a href="http://chnm.gmu.edu">Roy Rosenzweig Center for History and New Media</a> (RRCHNM) at George Mason University, the <em>Archive</em> is contributing to the on-going effort by historians and archivists to record and preserve the record of 9/11 by collecting and archiving first-hand accounts, emails and other electronic communications, digital photographs and artworks, and a range of other digital materials related to the attacks. The <em>Archive</em> is also using these events as a way of assessing how history is being recorded and preserved in the twenty-first century and as an opportunity to develop free software tools to help historians to do a better job of collecting, preserving, and writing history in the new century.</p>
<p>Our goal is to create a permanent record of the events of September 11, 2001. To these ends the <em>Archive</em> has partnered with the Library of Congress, which in September 2003 accepted a copy of the <em>Archive</em> into its permanent collections – an event that both ensured the <em>Archive</em>’s long-term preservation and marked the Library’s first major digital acquisition.</p>
<p>In 2011, the project received a <a href="https://www.nps.gov/preservation-grants/sat/">Save America's Treasures Grant</a>, from the <a href="http://nps.gov">National Park Service</a> and the <a href="http://neh.gov">National Endowment for the Humanities</a>, to ensure its long-term perservation. During this grant, RRCHNM migrated the <em>Archive</em> to the<em> Omeka</em> software and relaunched the website on a more stable platform.</p>
<p>Through maintaining these collections, we hope to foster some positive legacies of those terrible events by allowing people to tell their stories, making those stories available to a wide audience, providing historical context for understanding those events and their consequences, and helping historians and archivists improve their practices based on the lessons we learn from this project.</p>
<p>Regarding the rights management of materials in the archive, please be aware that the original contributors maintain copyright over all objects. We are no longer able to provide a referral service to secure these rights. </p>
<p>You can <a href="https://rrchnm.org/our-story/contact-us/">contact this project</a> through the Roy Rosenzweig Center for History and New Media.</p>

<h2>Finding old content</h2>
<p>In 2014, the September 11 Digital Archive moved to a new underlying system. This changed the links for many of the items in our collection. </p>
<p>If you have an old url which looks something like this: http://911digitalarchive.org/stories/details/10145, that url will no longer work. </p>
<p>However, you can use the old url to find the content in the current site. The "stories" in the old url indicates that the item is part of the <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20211123222248/https://911digitalarchive.org/collections/show/23">Digital Archive Stories Collection</a> - you might also see "<a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20211123222248/https://911digitalarchive.org/collections/show/27">art</a>" or "images".</p>
<p>The name of that item in the new system will be the kind of thing it is - story, art, image - and the number from the old url. So what had been at <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20211123222248/https://web.archive.org/web/20040210032507/http://911digitalarchive.org/stories/details/10145">http://911digitalarchive.org/stories/details/10145 [Internet Archive view]</a> can be found at the item <a href="http://web.archive.org/web/20211123222248/https://911digitalarchive.org/items/show/6782">Story10145.xml</a></p>
