        I was sitting in my English classroom, just going through my regular routine; little did I know that it wasn't going to be just a regular routine that day.  A few upperclassmen come in and tell my teacher to quickly turn the television on.  She told them she would later and that we were busy.  But I saw it through the window of another classroom: the air attack on the Twin Towers in New York.  It didn't seem real at all.  It looked like some special graphics from a movie or something but it was real.  And it hit a little too close to home for me.

        I am the type of person that hates to hear of people dying, whether I know them or not, but I usually never cry.  But as I said; that day wasn't going to be just a regular routine.  I usually don't pray for God's blessings upon the souls of those lost; but it was a different situation.  I dealt with crying, praying, and patriotism for weeks and it was like an experience I've never felt before.  I was scared and proud at the same time.  Scared that it could happen again, proud of those brave souls who helped crash a plane so it couldn't make it to its Washington D.C. destination.  I also prayed that I could be more like them if I ever had to be in such a situation.
