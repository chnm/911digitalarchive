        Tuesday, March 19, 2002 6:16 PM
        Government's 9/11 Fund Exclusions


        To the Bush Administration and 9/11 Fund Administrator Feinberg:

        I am disgusted, angered, and disappointed by the recently announced decision
        to exclude surviving partners of lesbian and gay victims of the 9/11
        attacks.  That this exclusion is cloaked in an ostensible reliance on state
        laws is a transparent attempt to deflect responsibiltity by harking to a
        kind of "state's rights" interpretation - "if they don't recognize 'em, we
        won't."

        Mr. Feinberg in a television appearance stressed that with a short life to
        the fund - 1 1/2 to 2 years - he is not able to follow any path BUT these
        particular state laws (though the same is not true of other laws and
        regulations, for example those dealing with immigrants).  I would suggest
        that it is _exactly_ the limited nature of this fund's life and of his
        mandate that creates a perfect case for NOT discriminating among victims -
        to determine whose life is, in the case of this fund, literally worth more
        to the survivors.  There can be little rational complaint about "social
        engineering" or charges of an effort to change state laws when the focus of
        this fund and its time frame are so limited.

        In addition, treating the survivors of _all_ victims the same would allow
        the Bush administration to act upon its rhetoric of U.S. unity and to model
        equality in the wake of these attacks.  Because, make no mistake, I am not
        suggesting that anyone Mr. Feinberg has already "defined in" be excluded;
        rather, I want you to treat all survivors the same and bring in those he
        chose to leave out.

        As I understand it, that would be the American Way.


        Individual Comment
        Minneapolis, Minnesota
