        I'm a coordinator for the N.Y. Board of Elections. On the morning of 9/11, while working at my poll site in a
        synagogue, the police officer informed me of the planes
        that had crashed into the WTC. At first, I didn't know
        what to think about it but a few minutes later a young
        women entered the synagogue and stated that another plane
        hit the Pentagon. I was starting to feel fear, I didn't
        know exactly what was going on, but I knew that I had to
        contact my family. I called home and one of my sons
        answered and I told him to turn on the T.V. and he informed
        me that the towers collapsed. That did it for me. I was
        totally in limbo. All I kept thinking about was my kids.
        Since the city was shut down, I walked home, it took
        about 2 hours. I picked up my younger children in school
        and bursted into tears when I saw them. They could not
        understand what was going on.
        What hurts me most of all, is the fact that those 2
        beautiful buildings are gone. I had the pleasure of being
        able to work in Tower 1 on the 53rd floor about 10 years
        ago. I also knew one of the victims, her name was
        Nicole Lindo she was on the 103rd floor of Tower 1. Her
        remains were never recovered.
        The events of 9/11 has left me with great sadness. It
        will take me many years to get over this.
