      text: I was getting ready for work and turned on the TV to check the stock market.  There was an image of a burning building and I thought I was on the wrong channel, but I wasn't. I turned up the volume and learned a plane had crashed into the WTC.  I was stunned, but assumed it was a horrible accident.  A woman was on the street talking when she basically went hysterical.  A second plane had hit the other building.  I was stunned.  I couldn't take it in, I started actually getting physical ill as the reality sunk in.  Someone had declared war on us and was using our own planes as weapons.  I frantically tried to calculate how many planes were over the US at any given moment, a hundred? a thousand? more?  I called my mother and told her to turn on her television.  Then the Pentagon was hit.  I started to cry.  My daughter was in the suburbs of NYC, was she OK?  The phone lines were busy.  People were jumping from the WTC buildings.  I saw their falling lives that had mere seconds of life left.  Everything was spinning out of control.  And then they collapsed.  Something inside me turned off.  If I took in any more I would not be able to withstand the pain.  I filled every container in my house with water and mechanically went to work and did my job.  It was something to do.  Anything not to have to deal with reality.  A plane had crashed in Pennsylvania came the frantic whispers from co-workers.  Oh God, I prayed, please make it stop.  That night I was glued to the TV as the videos played the nightmare over and over again.  And I did have nightmares.  Every night for two weeks.  My daughter was OK, but America would never be the same.  I am thankful for the safety of my family.  I mourn the loss of all those magnificent lives lost that day.  I can never say thank you enough for all the people that risked their lives to save others that day.  I still look up whenever a plane goes overhead, but we survived.  God bless the USA.
