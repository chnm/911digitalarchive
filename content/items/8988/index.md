        <p> <b>TOWERS OF WORDS: THE PLACE OF POETRY IN CRISES</b>&lt;/p align&gt;<br>
        &nbsp;As a human, I watched the Towers implode from my office window on<br>
        Duane Street, six blocks from ground zero. I thought, - What to do? My<br>
        response was to write a poem. And I wasn't alone. Towards the hole in<br>
        the energy center, towards the sacred burial site of steel, concrete<br>
        and ash, words began to emerge, looking for meaning, mourning,<br>
        attempting to understand. Words on Missing Posters, words on makeshift<br>
        memorials, scrawled on post-its and written in the dust on the windows<br>
        of J&amp;R Music, on the canvases at Union Square, in emails.<br>
        &nbsp;The impulse to build a Twin Towers of Words, to create a poem<br>
        replicating/ remembering what had been blasted by humanity's failings,<br>
        a response of art, this came later. The word went out that a Tower was<br>
        being built at the People's Poetry Gathering website<br>
        (www.peoplespoetry.org). The Gathering, a biennial festival of<br>
        international poetries sponsored by City Lore and Poets House, is a<br>
        place where all traditions of poetry, written and spoken, are<br>
        celebrated, where connections are made and understanding is meaning. It<br>
        was a perfect site for poets to post their hearts, and they did, often<br>
        leaving whole poems rather than simply adding a line. So I wove lines<br>
        together, creating a single poem out of the many submissions. 110 lines<br>
        were selected, one for each floor in the Towers, from more than 150<br>
        submissions. Steve Zeitlin and Joe Dobkin helped with the creation.<br>
        &nbsp;The second tower was invitational-I sent out 130 invites, and most<br>
        poets responded positively. Kathleen Masterson responded very<br>
        positively-she was the first poet to submit a line. Eileen Myles edited<br>
        some lines, including the first one, instead of writing her own.<br>
        &nbsp;Poets were invited to place their lines wherever they wished, so the<br>
        poem kept changing, evolving into itself. About halfway through, my<br>
        hard drive crashed and I lost all the names-very embarrassing to have<br>
        to ask which brilliant line was whose. Robert Kelly wrote back, "Who<br>
        cares? Aren't we all the poet? It sure is all the same crisis anyhow."<br>
        The poets range from the wellknown to the unknown, from teenagers to<br>
        septuagenarians, text, hiphop, language, avant garde, formalists.<br>
        College professors, homeless poets, politicos, cynics. Again, Steve<br>
        Zeitlin and Joe Dobkin helped form the poem.<br>
        <p><i>&nbsp;In a crisis<br>
         &nbsp; &nbsp;In times of crisis<br>
        &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp;Poets lose words<br>
         &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp;They loose them<br>
        &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; Find them here<br>
         &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp;Find some</i><br>
         &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp;-Bob Holman</p>
